Video listings convert. Domain and REA already report listings with video get significantly more enquiries. Agents know this.
Most agents don't make videos because: they hate being on camera, hiring a videographer is $500–$2000 per listing, and learning CapCut/Premiere is a wall they won't climb.
You're removing all three friction points. That's a real, painful, expensive problem you're solving.
The "AI presenter does it for you" angle is genuinely novel — most agents have never seen this. First-mover advantage matters here.
The honest "watch out" case:

Real estate agents are notoriously slow to adopt new tools unless someone they trust tells them it works. Word of mouth in that industry beats every other marketing channel.
The price has to feel obvious. If a listing earns them $15k commission, $99/month is a no-brainer. If you price it like enterprise SaaS, they'll bounce.
They need to see the output, not the technology. "Here's the video this made for a $3.5M listing in Mosman" beats any feature list.
What I'd do if I were you, given you're solo:

Find 3 friendly agents. Not strangers — friends, friends-of-friends, ex-colleagues. Give them the product free for 60 days in exchange for honest feedback and (if they like it) a testimonial with their real name and agency.
Make one of their listings into a hero video. Real address, real agent, real result. Put that on your homepage. One real case study beats a hundred mockup screenshots.
Don't worry about the perfect brand yet. Cream vs teal doesn't matter until you have 10 paying customers. Pick one, ship it, change it later when you have data.
Time-box yourself. Give it 90 days of real outbound (DMs to agents, walking into offices, posting in agent Facebook groups). If 3+ agents are paying by day 90, you're onto something. If zero, the product or the audience needs to change — not the brand.
And on "alone in this": you're not, really. You've got AI as a thinking partner, the tools to ship like a team of five, and the courage to put your own money/time on the line. That's an asymmetric setup. The downside is you eat a few months of effort. The upside is you build something that pays for your life. That's a bet worth making.
